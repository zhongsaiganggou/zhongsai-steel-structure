Nigeria Climate & Design Considerations

Nigeria has a tropical monsoon climate with distinct wet (April-October) and dry (November-March) seasons. High humidity (60-90%), heavy rainfall (1,500-3,000mm annually in south, less in north), and high temperatures (25-35°C year-round). Coastal areas (Lagos, Port Harcourt) have salt-spray corrosion risk. Key design: corrosion-resistant coatings for humid tropical climate, steep roof pitches for rainwater runoff, thermal insulation, proper ventilation, and elevated foundation design for flood-prone areas (especially Lagos coastal regions).

Wind Load & Seismic Design for Nigeria

Basic wind speed: 30-40 m/s (coastal areas higher, especially Lagos). Seismic activity: low (Nigeria is not in a major seismic zone, but minor tremors occur in some regions). Design per Nigerian Building Code (NBC): NBC 2006 (based on British Standards), EBCS (Eurocode-based) also accepted. Wind load per NBC clause 6.2. Key features: proper bracing for wind loads, roof uplift resistance (especially important for coastal storms), and column base plate design. For Lagos coastal projects, we design for higher wind speeds and add storm surge considerations for foundations.

Nigeria Building Codes & SONCAP Certification

Nigeria uses NBC (Nigerian Building Code, 2006), largely based on British Standards (BS 5950 for steel, BS 6399 for loads). Eurocode (EN 1993) is also increasingly accepted. SONCAP (Standards Organization of Nigeria Conformity Assessment Program) is MANDATORY for all regulated imports including steel structures. Process: Product Certificate (PC, valid 1 year) + per-shipment SONCAP Certificate (SC). ZhongSai includes both in quotations. Import duty: 10-15% (varies by HS code). VAT: 7.5%. Additional levies: 0.5% ITF, 0.2% NSITF. Form M (import declaration) required before shipment. A COREN-registered local engineer must review/stamp drawings for permit.

Major Ports & Shipping to Nigeria

Major ports: Apapa Port (Lagos) — largest, most congested, general cargo; Tin Can Island Port (Lagos) — alternative to Apapa, less congested; Onne Port (Port Harcourt) — oil/gas + general cargo, serves southeast; Calabar Port — southeast, free trade zone; Warri Port — Delta. Shipping from Shenzhen to Lagos (Apapa/Tin Can): 30-40 days (direct or transship via Singapore/Durban). To Onne: 32-42 days. Port congestion is a major issue — Apapa often has 3-7 day waiting time. Demurrage: 50-150 USD/day/container after free days (7-14). We recommend Tin Can Island for general cargo and pre-clearing customs before ship arrival to minimize demurrage.

⏱️ Typical Delivery Time to Nigeria

Production: 4-6 weeks. SONCAP: 1-2 weeks (overlap). Shipping to Lagos: 30-40 days. Port congestion: 3-7 days. Customs clearance: 7-14 days. Inland transport: 2-7 days. Total from order to site: 10-14 weeks. This is longer than Southeast Asia (6-9 weeks) due to distance and port congestion. Plan buffer time for potential delays.

Recommended Steel Grades & Corrosion Protection

Primary steel: Q355B (equivalent to BS EN 10025 S355) for main frames, Q235B for secondary. Nigeria humid tropical + coastal corrosion requires robust protection. System: Shop primer: epoxy zinc-rich (80μm). Intermediate: epoxy micaceous iron oxide (120μm) — extra thickness for humidity. Finish: polyurethane (60μm). Total DFT: 260μm. For coastal (Lagos, Port Harcourt, within 10km of sea): add hot-dip galvanizing for all secondary members, increase total DFT to 320μm, and use epoxy polyamide primer. All materials packed with desiccants and waterproof wrapping for 30-40 day ocean voyage.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is SONCAP certification mandatory for steel structures?

Yes. SONCAP is mandatory for all regulated products imported into Nigeria, including steel structures (HS 7308). Without a valid SONCAP Certificate (SC), customs will not clear the shipment, and you may face fines, storage charges, demurrage, or even seizure/re-export. ZhongSai provides both PC (Product Certificate) and SC (per-shipment) as part of our standard service at no extra cost.

What is the total landed cost for a steel warehouse in Nigeria?

For a 1,000 sqm standard warehouse: FOB China 70,000-100,000 USD, shipping 14,000-20,000 USD (4 containers to Lagos), SONCAP 500-1,200 USD, import duty 10-15% (7,000-15,000 USD), VAT 7.5% (6,000-9,000 USD), other levies 1,000-2,000 USD, inland transport 1,000-3,000 USD, customs broker 500-1,500 USD, potential demurrage 500-3,000 USD. Total landed: approximately 100,500-154,700 USD. Add foundation 15,000-30,000 USD and installation 10,000-25,000 USD for total project cost.

How do you handle port congestion in Lagos?

We recommend several strategies: 1) Use Tin Can Island Port as an alternative to Apapa when congestion is severe. 2) Pre-clear customs documentation before the ship arrives (start Form M and SONCAP process immediately after order). 3) Book shipping 2 weeks in advance to secure space. 4) Use an experienced Nigerian customs broker with good port relationships. 5) Budget for potential demurrage and storage charges (500-3,000 USD contingency). 6) For large projects, consider splitting shipments to reduce port congestion impact.

What building standards are accepted in Nigeria?

Nigeria primarily uses the Nigerian Building Code (NBC 2006), which is based on British Standards (BS 5950 for steel design, BS 6399 for loads). Eurocode 3 (EN 1993) is also increasingly accepted, especially for international projects. We provide complete calculation packages in English, and a COREN-registered local structural engineer must review and stamp the drawings for building permit approval. We work with local engineers to ensure smooth compliance.
